# Exercise 2. Distance as the Length of a Vector

## Question

The points are $A=(1,2,3)$ and $B=(4,-2,6)$.

1. Construct $\overrightarrow{AB}$.
2. Compute its length.
3. Compute the distance between the points using the coordinate formula.
4. Explain why both calculations give the same result.

## Solution

The vector from $A$ to $B$ is "end minus start":

$$
\overrightarrow{AB}=B-A=(4-1,\ -2-2,\ 6-3)=(3,-4,3)
$$

Its length:

$$
\|\overrightarrow{AB}\|=\sqrt{3^2+(-4)^2+3^2}=\sqrt{34}
$$

The distance formula for two points gives:

$$
d(A,B)=\sqrt{(4-1)^2+(-2-2)^2+(6-3)^2}=\sqrt{34}
$$

The results are the same because the distance formula uses exactly the differences of the coordinates, and these differences are the coordinates of $\overrightarrow{AB}$. So the distance between two points is just the length of the vector between them.
